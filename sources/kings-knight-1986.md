- https://wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Knight#Development_and_release -- Remaster (1986) version* -- accessed 2026-10-14
  - (section text pasted by user)
  - "Differences between versions": the Japanese Famicom and American NES versions differ little (only the title screen, which carries the copyright and publisher)
  - the MSX version differs more: a controller choice (keyboard or joystick) before the intro, the screen's flanks closed off, stage maps and items shifted slightly, and it may seem easier because the characters take more damage
  - the MSX port has more sound channels with altered or improved music and effects, a wider range of colors, updated graphics, new monster and boss designs (dragons at the cave ends replaced by reptilian monsters) and a new sidebar with the life meter, power-ups and elements
  - it could not scroll vertically as smoothly, so movement is choppy
  - similar to the changes when Final Fantasy was ported to the MSX2
  - This agrees with the entry's Remaster (1986) description
    - the section says nothing about the 1987 PC-8801 and Sharp X1 "King's Knight Special", so that version's relationship to the Famicom original or the MSX remaster is still unresolved
- https://wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Knight#Remake -- Remake (2017) version* -- accessed 2026-10-14 (section text pasted by user)
  - King's Knight: Wrath of the Dark Dragon, a 2017 remake for iOS and Android, produced by Daisuke Motohashi and developed by Flame Hearts, a mobile spin-off associated with Final Fantasy XV
    - it keeps the original's scrolling action RPG style, adds a new camera perspective and an orchestral version of the chiptune soundtrack, and is free-to-play with a four-player multiplayer mode
  - Announced at the September 2016 Tokyo Game Show with promotional material placing it in the Final Fantasy XV universe (alongside Justice Monsters Five)
    - planned for Android and iOS in Japan in 2016, no North American announcement at the time
    - postponed to 2017
    - released worldwide on September 13 (2017) and shut down on June 26, 2018, less than a year later
  - Languages are not stated; a worldwide release makes more than Japanese likely, so the entry's unknown-languages marker on this version stands

ja.wikipedia.org (Information)
- https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/キングスナイト -- Edit (1987) version*, dates*, versions* -- accessed 2026-10-14
  - (page text pasted by user)
  - the original is the 1986-09-18 Famicom game from Square (developed by Workss, music Nobuo Uematsu; US 1989)
  - the ports table: MSX (Square, Japan 1986-11, ROM cartridge), キングスナイト・スペシャル (Japan 1987-06, PC-8801mkIISR and X1, developed by Square's Planning and Development Department, published by Square, floppy disk), Wii Virtual Console (Japan 2007-11-27, US 2008-03-24), 3DS Virtual Console (Japan 2014-02-05), Wii U Virtual Console (Japan 2016-07-06), all of the console ones Famicom ports
  - On the Special version: "like the MSX version, an information window on the right of the screen
    - a labyrinth added to each stage, various gimmicks and items not in the Famicom version, puzzles to solve, a stronger RPG content
    - visual scenes added
    - the package illustration is by the animator Norio Shioyama"
    - an MSX2 version and an FM-7 "Special" were also developed but cancelled -- this settles the version's earlier "unclear" lineage: an expanded edition that keeps the MSX sidebar
  - On the MSX version: the same basic content as the Famicom with slightly different maps, two-colour characters, a right-hand information window, a 4-dot scrolling claim in ads that in practice stalled the game each scroll, a continue function and bugs, the same package illustration as the Famicom
  - Related books: the Asuka Shinsha "まんが攻略本" (manga guide, October 1986, drawn by Rei Ōyama) retells the game from prologue to ending as a manga with each hero's backstory, and "キングスナイト クレア姫を救え！" (a gamebook by MD Club, Fusosha's Square Gamebooks, 1986-06-02) is an original story with Rayjack and some game characters
    - also a "perfect guide"
    - none is on our list
    - whether the same reasoning as the 1999 Hirano manga (see Decisions) applies to them is not decided
  - The Famitsu article cited for the XV link is titled "'FFXV's Noctis and friends get hooked on it too!? Square's Famicom classic King's Knight becomes a smartphone RPG [TGS 2016]'"
    - external links include the Wrath of the Dark Dragon official site (dead), the three Virtual Console pages and MobyGames

## Decisions

- Not listing the 1999 manga Susume!! Seigaku Dennou Kenkyuubu (Kouta Hirano, Gamest Comics): the retcon into the Final Fantasy XV universe belongs to the 2017 mobile game, King's Knight itself is already only a tie-in curiosity, and the manga is a computer-club comedy covering several games, not an adaptation (user, 2026-10-14)
- Remake (2017) languages (user, 2026-10-14): only English is confirmed (US App Store)
  - a worldwide mobile release probably had several, so `helpWanted` stays on that version beside the unknown-languages marker
  - to check: other App Store storefronts and Google Play language lists in archived copies
- Language checks tried by the user 2026-10-14, nothing found: Wayback captures of the Japan App Store page, the Japanese Google Play page and other App Store storefronts, and a Google search for the supported languages
  - only the US App Store's "English" is known
- Primary entry languages (user, 2026-10-14): the original shipped in Japan and North America only (ja.wikipedia: Famicom 1986, US 1989; Wii Virtual Console Japan 2007 and US 2008, 3DS and Wii U Japan only), so JA and EN look complete, but the Virtual Console releases may have added languages
  - left as is, with the unknown-languages marker and `helpWanted` unchanged
- Scope (user, 2026-11-10): the King's Knight game and its remakes are in scope; works related to King's Knight are not; the manga Susume!! Seigaku Dennou Kenkyuubu (Kouta Hirano, Gamest Comics, April 1999), named in en.wikipedia's King's Knight article, section Other media (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Knight#Other_media), is omitted
- Omitted (same scope as the King's Knight manga): the two Asukashinsha guidebooks and the gamebook of the 関連書籍 section above; they are books related to King's Knight, not the game or its remakes
