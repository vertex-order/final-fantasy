## Decisions

- **Not to be listed: Final Fantasy XIV: Libra Eorzea (user, 2026-10-20):** a free smartphone app by Square Enix (ファイナルファンタジーXIV: ライブラ エオルゼア), iOS 2013-09-10 and Android 2013-11-01 in all regions, giving offline access to the official XIV database and a character profile viewer for gear and attributes; discontinued 2019-04-23 and supplanted by the Final Fantasy XIV Companion
  - A different app, not a version of the Companion: its own store listing (iOS id 682415504, the Companion is id 1293636320) and a different function (an offline reference database and profile viewer, not the Companion's chat, market board and subscription service)
  - It does not meet the content bar: the database is encyclopedia-like content and the profile viewer is too minor a tool for XIV players; and as a discontinued app there is nothing left to load, and no experience to gain from an old build or old videos of it
  - The Companion stays (it barely meets the bar, but it is an important tool and the subscription service for XIV players)
  - Evidence: https://breezewiki.com/finalfantasy/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIV:_Libra_Eorzea (the Final Fantasy Wiki page, pasted by user 2026-10-20) and the official end-of-service notice, http://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/pr/special/libraeorzea/ (still live; "As of April 23, 2019, service for FINAL FANTASY XIV: Libra Eorzea will be discontinued", pointing to the Companion and the Lodestone database)
  - Found on the Final Fantasy Wiki's iOS and iPadOS page (see the apps.apple.com section above) and in a 2014 Wayback capture of the Square Enix developer page on the App Store
- **Not to be listed: three more items from the Final Fantasy Wiki's iOS and iPadOS page (user, 2026-10-20):**
  - Distant Worlds: Music from Final Fantasy: an iOS app listed under the wiki's Applications, tied to the Distant Worlds concert series; excluded because it is music only
  - Square Enix Software Token: a security token (authenticator) app for Square Enix accounts; excluded: it is a useful tool for XIV players, but XIV's login page already steers players to two-factor authentication, so players are told about it, and people who don't play XIV have no need to discover it
  - Dive In (service): Square Enix's subscription cloud-streaming service in Japan, launched 2014-10-09 and shut down 2015-09-13, run on G-Cluster's framework to stream games to iOS, iPadOS and Android devices, each with a free 30-minute trial and timed access passes; excluded as a distribution platform (a Japan-only, short-lived one), the games stay listed on their own rows
    - Final Fantasy games on it, per the Final Fantasy Wiki: VII International, VIII, XIII and XIII-2 (dates and rows are in their sources files)
    - https://breezewiki.com/finalfantasy/wiki/G-Cluster (pasted by user 2026-10-20): G-Cluster was a Japan-only cloud gaming service (a G-Cluster device for HDTVs plus game-specific streaming apps on iOS, iPadOS and Android); Square Enix used parts of its technology for Dive In; founded in Finland in 2000, the global company went bankrupt and ended its service on 2016-05-23, and Japan's Broadmedia kept the platform until it was retired on 2025-02-28
    - Cloud services in Japan that carried FF games (user, 2026-10-22). This file keeps only what applies to a whole service; each title's dates, links and details are in its own sources file: final-fantasy-vii-1997.md, final-fantasy-viii-1999.md, final-fantasy-ix-2000.md, final-fantasy-x-2001.md, final-fantasy-x-2-2003.md, final-fantasy-xiii-2009.md, final-fantasy-xiii-2-2011.md, lightning-returns-final-fantasy-xiii-2013.md and final-fantasy-xv-2016.md
    - G-cluster (Broadmedia): a Japan-only cloud game service. The technology belonged to G-cluster Global Inc. (the 2013 to 2016 releases); later releases are by Broadmedia GC Inc., a Broadmedia subsidiary. The console went on sale on 2013-06-20 (https://ad.impress.co.jp/special/g-cluster1306/, an advertorial: a 45 g HDMI box played with a USB pad or a phone as the controller, with the T's TV video-rental service on the same box).
