- **Not to be listed: Knights of the Crystals (user, 2026-10-23):** a Square Enix social RPG for GREE phones in Japan (ナイツ オブ クリスタル, Japan 2010-08-10), on Facebook worldwide from 2010-11-02 and on iOS and Android from 2011-09-01, closed 2012-02-29; branded separately, not as a Final Fantasy title
  - It borrows the series' look: jobs (Warrior, Monk, White and Black Mage, Dark Knight, Dragoon), classic summons (Ifrit, Shiva, Bahamut), monsters such as the Lamia and the Imp, Final Fantasy lettering and a crystal in its logo, and a Final Fantasy Tactics-themed job card set (August 2011); illustrated by Ryoma Ito (Final Fantasy Tactics Advance)
  - No official tie: the Final Fantasy Wiki page says it "was not formally released as a Final Fantasy title" and files it under Category: Related games (46 pages, mixing Dragon Quest, Persona 5 Royal, Fall Guys and other publishers' games); MobyGames, the game's own wiki and Kotaku do not mention a Final Fantasy link; Wikipedia's List of Final Fantasy video games, "Other games", does not list it
  - Its closest comparisons are Final Fantasy Adventure and The Final Fantasy Legend I–III, which stay listed because they were marketed as Final Fantasy games ("Final Fantasy" is in the title, so the list's hand is forced; the Legend games are one omnibus entry); Knights of the Crystals never was. Other Square Enix titles that are not Final Fantasy are already out of scope (see the Dragon Quest, SaGa and Mana line above)
  - Evidence: https://breezewiki.com/finalfantasy/wiki/Knights_of_the_Crystals (pasted by user 2026-10-23), https://www.4gamer.net/games/123/G012325/20101102028/ (the Facebook launch of 2010-11-02, Square Enix's own wording; official sites http://www.square-enix.co.jp/facebook/koc/ and apps.facebook.com/knightsofthecrystals/), https://kotaku.com/knights-of-the-crystals-is-final-fantasy-for-facebook-5679450, https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/KnightsOfTheCrystals, https://www.mobygames.com/game/52030/knights-of-the-crystals/
- **Not to be listed: Chocobo Racing 3D (user, 2026-11-03):** the whole game is left out, not a platform. A Nintendo 3DS sequel to Chocobo Racing announced at E3 2010, cancelled in 2013 (Square Enix, via Siliconera 2013-10-10) and explained in 2014 as a quality problem (Shinji Hashimoto, via Nintendo Life 2014-06-04); never released, and Chocobo GP (Nintendo Switch, 2022) is a separate game
  - Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cancelled_Nintendo_3DS_games (revision 1374058306 of 2026-09-09; Chocobo Racing 3D row); details in sources/chocobo-game-series-1997.md
